Now let's look at how a tag behaves for every person, input and setting. A good tag is [accessible](/docs/components/tag/ux#accessible), [predictable](/docs/components/tag/ux#predictable), designed in [every state](/docs/components/tag/ux#every-state) and [adaptive](/docs/components/tag/ux#adaptive).

## Accessible

### Keep a plain tag plain text

A tag that people can't remove or follow is a word on the page, so the Tag renders it as text in a `<span>`, with no role and no place in the tab order. A screen reader reads it where it sits, like the words around it, and keyboard users don't stop on something that does nothing when they get there. Carbon's read-only tags work the same way: they "are not in the tab order, are not interactive, and do not receive focus".

Only the parts people act on are interactive, and each is a real element: a link when the tag goes somewhere, and a button when it can be removed. A tag never gets `tabindex`, `role="button"` or a click handler on its root, since none of them would tell people what pressing it does.

### Name the remove button

A removable tag has a native `<button type="button">` after its label, so it's in the tab order, `Enter` and `Space` press it, and it never submits a form by accident. Its icon is hidden, so the button needs a name of its own, and the Tag builds it from the label, like "Remove Bug". That name is what people hear and say:

- A screen reader says "Remove Bug, button", rather than "button" or "×", so people know which tag it removes ([WCAG 2.4.6](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/headings-and-labels.html), [4.1.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/name-role-value.html)).
- Every remove button in a row has a name of its own, so voice control users can say "click Remove Bug", and people who list a page's buttons can tell them apart.
- `removeLabel` changes the wording, like for another language. Keep the tag's text in it, since that's what people see and say ([WCAG 2.5.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/label-in-name.html)).

The name comes from `label`, so a removable tag always needs one, even when its default slot shows something else, like a link. Our reference implementation warns you in the console when it's missing.

### Link a tag to its page

A tag can take people to a page about what it names, like the other posts on a topic. With `href`, the label holds a real `<a href>`, so a screen reader says "link, Accessibility", people can open it in a new tab, and they can see where it goes before they press it.

- A link tag looks different from a plain one, with a border, so people can tell which tags go somewhere without relying on colour ([WCAG 1.4.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/use-of-color.html)).
- Its link takes a press anywhere on the tag, and its name is still the label alone, since the leading icon is hidden.
- We recommend linking only to a page about what the tag names, like a topic's posts or a label's issues. [Carbon](https://carbondesignsystem.com/components/tag/usage/) says not to use tags "as links that direct you to an entirely different page", and a tag that leads somewhere else surprises people. [Atlassian's tags](https://atlassian.design/components/tag/usage) can be links too.
- A link for your router, or one that needs `target`, `rel` or `download`, goes in the default slot, which [Links and routers](/docs/components/tag/dx#links-and-routers) covers.

### Contrast and size

- The label needs at least 4.5:1 against the tag's background, and the focus ring at least 3:1 against what's behind it ([WCAG 1.4.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html), [1.4.11](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html)). Our tokens come to 12.3:1 on white and 10.9:1 on zinc-900, as [Tokens](/docs/components/tag/ui#tokens) shows.
- The remove button and a link each take a press over at least 24 × 24px, at every size, so people with less precise hands can hit them ([WCAG 2.5.8](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html)). At `xs` and `sm`, a tag is shorter than that, so the hit area reaches past it, the way the Select's tags do.
- On touch screens, both reach at least 44px tall, which [WCAG 2.5.5](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-enhanced.html) asks for at level AAA.

### Show focus

When its link has focus, the whole tag shows an outline in the same focus colour as every other component, 2px thick and 2px outside its edge ([WCAG 2.4.7](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-visible.html)). The remove button draws its outline inside its edge, as the Select's tags do, so the ring never covers the tag next to it. Only one of them can have focus at a time, so only one thing on the page ever looks focused.

## Predictable

### Keyboard

| Key                | Tag                                                                          |
| ------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `Tab`, `Shift+Tab` | Moves focus to the tag's link, then to its remove button. A plain or disabled tag isn't a stop |
| `Enter`            | Follows the link, or presses the remove button                               |
| `Space`            | Presses the remove button                                                    |

A tag has no keys of its own. It's a link and a button, which `Tab` reaches in the order they come in, like on the rest of the page. `Enter` follows the link, and `Enter` or `Space` press the remove button, as they dismiss [Carbon's dismissible tags](https://carbondesignsystem.com/components/tag/accessibility/). Keys like `Backspace` or `Delete` on a focused tag, or the arrow keys between tags, only make sense in a group of tags that has a keyboard model of its own, so the Tag Group, which comes next, will settle them.

### Move focus when a tag goes

The Tag doesn't remove itself. Pressing its remove button fires `remove`, and the page decides whether the tag goes, since it's the page that knows what the tag stands for, like a filter or a value in a form. Whatever removes it then has two jobs:

::tag-remove-example
::framework-switcher
#react
```tsx
const [filters, setFilters] = useState(ALL);
const list = useRef<HTMLUListElement>(null);
const reset = useRef<HTMLButtonElement>(null);

function remove(filter: string) {
  const index = filters.indexOf(filter);
  flushSync(() => setFilters(filters.filter((other) => other !== filter)));
  const buttons = list.current!.querySelectorAll<HTMLElement>("[data-slot=remove]");
  (buttons[index] ?? buttons[index - 1] ?? reset.current)?.focus();
  announce(`${filter} removed`); // through a polite live region
}

<ul ref={list} className="filters" aria-label="Filters">
  {filters.map((filter) => (
    <li key={filter}><Tag label={filter} removable onRemove={() => remove(filter)} /></li>
  ))}
</ul>
<Button ref={reset} size="xs" variant="ghost" onClick={() => setFilters(ALL)}>Reset</Button>
```

</script>

  selector: "app-filters",
  imports: [Tag, Button],
  template: `
    <ul #list class="filters" aria-label="Filters">
      @for (filter of filters(); track filter) {
        <li><span appTag [label]="filter" removable (remove)="remove(filter)"></span></li>
      }
    </ul>
    <button #reset appButton size="xs" variant="ghost" (click)="filters.set(all)">Reset</button>
  `,
})
export class Filters {
  all = ALL;
  filters = signal([...ALL]);
  list = viewChild.required<ElementRef<HTMLElement>>("list");
  reset = viewChild.required<ElementRef<HTMLElement>>("reset");
  injector = inject(Injector);

  remove(filter: string) {
    const index = this.filters().indexOf(filter);
    this.filters.update((all) => all.filter((other) => other !== filter));
    afterNextRender(() => {
      const buttons = this.list().nativeElement.querySelectorAll<HTMLElement>("[data-slot=remove]");
      (buttons[index] ?? buttons[index - 1] ?? this.reset().nativeElement).focus();
      announce(`${filter} removed`); // through a polite live region
    }, { injector: this.injector });
  }
}

- **Move focus.** The remove button had focus, and it's gone with its tag, so focus would drop to the top of the page, and keyboard users would lose their place ([WCAG 2.4.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-order.html)). Move it to the next tag's remove button, or the previous one when it was the last, or to something nearby when no tags are left.
- **Say what went.** A screen reader announces where focus lands, but not that a tag disappeared, so announce it through a polite live region, like "Bug removed" ([WCAG 4.1.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/status-messages.html)).

The Select does both for its own tags, which you'll find in `select/focus-after-remove` and `select/announce-removal`. The Tag Group, which comes next, will do them for any group of tags, so these aren't rules of the Tag's own.

### Don't nest interactive elements

The link and the remove button sit side by side in the tag, never one inside the other. HTML doesn't allow a button inside a link, or a link inside a button ([interactive content](https://html.spec.whatwg.org/multipage/dom.html#interactive-content)), so nothing defines what browsers and screen readers should do with one, and pressing the remove button might follow the link too. Carbon keeps it the same way, with the close icon as the only part of a dismissible tag to press.

### Keep labels short

A tag is a word or two, like a topic or a label's name, so people can take in a row of them at a glance. [Atlassian](https://atlassian.design/components/tag/usage) says to keep labels short, since its tags cut long ones off at 200px. Anything longer belongs in the text around the tags, or on the page the tag links to.

### Keep status out of tags

A tag says what something is about, while a status says what state it's in, like "Draft", "Overdue" or "3 unread". [Atlassian](https://atlassian.design/components/tag/usage) says the same of its tags: for "workflow status, system state, priority", it points you to its lozenge. A status goes in a Badge, on our [Roadmap](/docs/getting-started/roadmap), which nobody removes or follows, so the two never look like they mean the same thing.

## Every state

### Removable

A removable tag shows its remove button after its label, at the end edge, as wide as the tag is tall. Pressing it fires `remove` and nothing else, so the tag stays until whatever owns it takes it away, and it's that code that moves focus and announces it, as [Move focus when a tag goes](/docs/components/tag/ux#move-focus-when-a-tag-goes) explains.

### Linked

A link tag has a border, and on hover its background gets stronger and its label is underlined, which tells people it goes somewhere before they press it. On focus, it shows its focus ring around the whole tag. A link tag can be removable too, and its remove button sits above the link, so pressing it removes the tag rather than following the link.

### Disabled

A disabled tag is dimmed to 50%, drops its remove button and turns its link off: the `<a>` loses its `href`, so it can't be followed, and takes `role="link"` and `aria-disabled="true"`, so a screen reader still says it's a link that's turned off, as the [Button](/docs/components/button/ux#disabled) does with a link. It leaves the tab order, like a plain tag. WCAG doesn't ask for contrast on disabled controls ([WCAG 1.4.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html)), but it's best to say somewhere nearby why a tag can't be removed, since people can't find out from the tag.

### Read-only

A read-only tag keeps its usual look and drops its remove button, so people can read it but not remove it, like a label someone else added. Its link still works, since reading a tag includes following it. The [Select](/docs/components/select/ux#read-only) does the same with its tags when it's read-only.

### Long labels

A long label stays on one line and ends in an ellipsis, so a row of tags keeps its height, as the Select's tags do. The whole text stays in the DOM, so screen readers read all of it. Sighted people can't, though, so try to keep labels short enough not to be cut off, and give a tag that might be cut off a link to a page that shows its whole name.

## Adaptive

### Pointer, touch and pen

- Hover styles only apply to pointers that can actually hover (`@media (hover: hover)`), otherwise a tap would leave the tag looking hovered.
- On touch screens, the remove button and a link reach at least 44px tall, as the Select's tags do on coarse pointers (`@media (any-pointer: coarse)`).
- At `xs` and `sm`, those hit areas reach past the tag, so they can cover a tag on the next row. Leave room between rows of tags, which the Select already does and the Tag Group will do for you.

### Motion

The Tag doesn't move. If you'd like a tag to fade out when it's removed, wrap the animation in `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)`, so it only runs for people who haven't asked for less motion ([WCAG 2.3.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html)), and move focus once it's gone.

### More contrast

When someone asks for more contrast (`prefers-contrast: more`), as with Increase contrast on macOS and iOS, a link tag's border is drawn in the text colour at full strength, rather than at 55%. The label already passes 4.5:1, so it stays as it is.

### Forced colours

Windows contrast themes replace every colour with the user's own, and drop backgrounds and shadows. Every tag gets a real border in `CanvasText`, as the Select's tags do, and a link tag one in `LinkText`, so its boundary stays visible without its background. Focus is drawn with `outline`, in `Highlight`, and a disabled tag uses `GrayText` rather than opacity.

### Colour scheme

The tag's background and its border mix the text colour, which `--color--neutral-text` sets with `light-dark()`, so the Tag follows `color-scheme` and adapts to your light and dark themes on its own, as long as they set it.

### Text size and spacing

The tag's height is a minimum in `rem`, so it grows with its text instead of clipping it, at 200% text size ([WCAG 1.4.4](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/resize-text.html)) and with wider letter and word spacing ([WCAG 1.4.12](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/text-spacing.html)). It never gets wider than what holds it, so a long label ends in an ellipsis rather than overflowing it.

## FAQ

### Tag or something else?

A tag says what something is about, and people can remove it or follow it. For anything else, another component will do a better job:

| When you need to…                                              | Use                                                                   |
| -------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Label what something is about, like a topic or a picked value  | Tag                                                                   |
| Mark a status or a count, like "New" or "3"                    | Badge                                                                 |
| Turn one thing on or off, like a filter                        | A [toggle Button](/docs/components/button/ux#toggle-buttons)          |
| Pick any number of options out of a few                        | [Checkbox Group](/docs/components/checkbox-group)                     |
| Take people somewhere, from inside a sentence                  | Link                                                                  |

The Badge and the Link are on our [Roadmap](/docs/getting-started/roadmap). If you're coming from the [GOV.UK Design System](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/tag/), keep in mind that its tag is what we call a Badge: it shows "the status of something", and GOV.UK says not to make it a link or a button.

::accordion
:::accordion-item{label="Can a tag be selected?"}
Not the Tag. A tag that turns on and off, like a filter chip, is a toggle button, with `aria-pressed`, which the Button covers under [Toggle buttons](/docs/components/button/ux#toggle-buttons). Picking any number of options out of a few is a [Checkbox Group](/docs/components/checkbox-group). Whether a group of tags can be selected is a question for the Tag Group, which comes next.

:::accordion-item{label="Why isn't a plain tag focusable?"}
Because there's nothing to do with it. A stop in the tab order that does nothing makes keyboard users press `Tab` once more for every tag, and screen readers already read the tag's text where it sits. Only its link and its remove button take focus, since people act on them.
:::

:::accordion-item{label="Should `Backspace` remove a tag?"}
Not a tag on its own. `Backspace` and `Delete` only make sense where focus moves between tags with the arrow keys, like in a field of tags people type into, so it's the Tag Group's and the Select's call. The Select already removes its last tag with `Backspace` on its closed control.
:::
