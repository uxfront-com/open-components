Now let's look at how a dialog behaves for every person, input and setting. A good dialog is [accessible](/docs/components/dialog/ux#accessible), [predictable](/docs/components/dialog/ux#predictable), designed in [every state](/docs/components/dialog/ux#every-state) and [adaptive](/docs/components/dialog/ux#adaptive).

## Accessible

### Use the native element

A native `<dialog>`, opened with `showModal()`, gives you most of what a modal dialog needs for free. It goes in the top layer, above everything else, without a `z-index`. The rest of the page becomes inert, so people can't focus it, click it or read it with a screen reader. `Escape` asks to close it, it has the `dialog` role, and its `::backdrop` covers the page.

A `<div>` with `role="dialog"` and `aria-modal="true"` only gives you the role, so every one of those is one more thing to rebuild by hand. On top of that, `aria-modal` alone doesn't stop screen readers from reading the page behind it in every browser.

::framework-switcher
#react
```tsx
{/* Do: you get the top layer, an inert page, Escape and the role for free */}
<dialog ref={dialog} aria-labelledby="edit-profile-title">…</dialog>
<button type="button" onClick={() => dialog.current?.showModal()}>Edit profile</button>

{/* Don't: there's no top layer, inert page or Escape until you rebuild them */}
<div className="dialog" role="dialog" aria-modal="true" aria-labelledby="edit-profile-title">…</div>
```

#vue

<!-- Do: you get the top layer, an inert page, Escape and the role for free -->

<!-- Don't: there's no top layer, inert page or Escape until you rebuild them -->

Two things to keep in mind. Open a modal dialog with `showModal()`, never with `show()` or the `open` attribute, which both open it as a non-modal dialog that leaves the page behind it working. And don't add `tabindex` to the `<dialog>` itself, since it moves focus to a control inside it, not to the panel.

### Give every dialog a title

When focus moves into a dialog, screen readers announce its role, its name and its description, like "Edit profile, dialog, Your name and email address, as other people see them." The name comes from the title, which the Dialog links with `aria-labelledby`, so give every dialog a visible title that says what it's for ([WCAG 1.3.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/info-and-relationships.html), [2.4.6](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/headings-and-labels.html), [4.1.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/name-role-value.html)). A dialog without one is announced as just "dialog", and people have to read through it to find out why it opened.

The title is an `<h2>`, so screen reader users can also jump to it by heading. It's one level below the page's `<h1>`, which stays the page's own, whatever the dialog is about.

### Keep people inside

While the dialog is open, the page behind it is inert. `Tab` and `Shift+Tab` move between the dialog's own controls, and past the last one, on to the browser's own interface, like the address bar, but never to the page ([WCAG 2.4.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-order.html)). Screen readers can't read the page either, so their virtual cursor stays in the dialog, and a click outside it lands on the backdrop. That's the whole point of a modal dialog, and `showModal()` does it for you.

It isn't a keyboard trap, though, since `Escape` and the close button always lead out ([WCAG 2.1.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/no-keyboard-trap.html)).

### Contrast and size

- The title, the description and the content need a contrast of at least 4.5:1 against the panel ([WCAG 1.4.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html)).
- The panel's border doesn't need 3:1, since the panel isn't a control and the backdrop already sets it apart. It's there for dark pages, where the backdrop barely shows.
- The close button is a `sm` Button, 32 × 32px, above the 24 × 24px minimum ([WCAG 2.5.8](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html)), and the actions are `md` Buttons, as everywhere else.

## Predictable

### Move focus in, and back out

When a dialog opens, focus moves inside it, so keyboard and screen reader users start right where the dialog asks for their attention ([WCAG 2.4.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-order.html)). The Dialog moves it, in this order, to:

1. The control you mark with `data-autofocus`.
2. Or else, the first control in its body, like the first input of a form.
3. Or else, its first action, which in an alert dialog is the one that leaves things as they are.
4. Or else, its close button.

The close button comes last, even though it comes first on the page, since starting there would mean tabbing past it to do anything. Mark a control with `data-autofocus` when the first one isn't where people should start, like the email address in a sign-in dialog that opens with a "Continue with Google" button:

::framework-switcher
#react
```tsx
<Dialog id="sign-in" title="Sign in" open={open} onClose={() => setOpen(false)}>
  <Button variant="outline">Continue with Google</Button>
  <label htmlFor="email">Email address</label>
  {/* Most people sign in with their email address, so it starts there */}
  <Input id="email" type="email" autoComplete="email" data-autofocus />
</Dialog>
```

<Dialog id="sign-in" title="Sign in" :open="open" @close="open = false">

We use `data-autofocus` rather than `autofocus`, since it works the same in every framework, while React focuses an `autoFocus` element as it renders, before the dialog is open. Browsers honour `autofocus` in a dialog too, so the Dialog counts it as well.

When the dialog closes, focus goes back to the control that opened it, so people carry on where they left off. Sometimes that control is gone, like a Delete button on a row the dialog just deleted. Focus then has nowhere to go, and falls back to the top of the page, so move it yourself, to the next row or the list, as the Button's [Move focus where people expect it](/docs/components/button/ux#move-focus-where-people-expect-it) explains.

### Keyboard

| Key                 | Dialog                                                                            |
| ------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `Tab`, `Shift+Tab`  | Move between its controls, and never to the page behind it                        |
| `Escape`            | Asks to close it, in an alert dialog too                                          |
| `Enter`, `Space`    | Press the focused Button, and `Enter` submits a form inside it                    |

`Escape` belongs to the control that has focus first. A control inside a dialog that closes on `Escape` too, like a menu, closes itself and stops it there, so one press doesn't close both.

### Close it every way people expect

Every way a dialog closes fires `close`, with a reason that says which:

| Reason         | When                                                                                   |
| -------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `escape`       | People press `Escape`, or make another close request, like the back gesture on Android |
| `close-button` | People press the close button                                                          |
| `backdrop`     | People press the backdrop, outside the panel, which an alert dialog ignores            |
| `action`       | People send a `<form method="dialog">` inside it, with the value of its button          |
| `closed`       | The browser has already closed it, which you can't turn down                           |

The Dialog asks, rather than closing itself. It stays open until you set `open` to false, which you'll usually do as soon as `close` fires. That way, the dialog on the screen and the state in your code stay in step, and you can say no, like when people would lose what they've typed. A dialog with unsaved changes should ask before it closes, rather than throw them away, so in that case, open an alert dialog that asks whether to discard them, and keep the first one open until people answer.

There's one case where you can't say no. A page can only turn down a close request, like the back gesture on Android, if people have pressed or typed something since the last one, so a second back gesture in a row closes the dialog. The Dialog then fires `close` with the reason `closed`, since the dialog is already gone, and gives focus back. Set `open` to false for `closed`, even in a handler that keeps the dialog open for every other reason, or `open` and the screen will disagree.

A press on the backdrop only counts when it starts there too. People often select text in a dialog and let go of the button outside it, and that shouldn't close it.

## Every state

### Open

While a dialog is open, it's the only thing on the page people can use. The page behind it is inert, and it doesn't scroll either. Scrolling the page under a dialog would move what people came back to, so the Dialog stops it, and keeps the space its scrollbar took, so the page doesn't shift sideways when it goes.

Keep to one dialog at a time. A dialog that opens another one, like the "Discard changes?" above, is fine, as long as it's on top and closes first, but a stack of them is hard to follow, and harder still with a screen reader.

### Closed

A closed dialog is hidden, so it isn't in the accessibility tree, and screen readers, agents and `Tab` can't find anything in it. The Dialog stays in the page while it's closed, so a form inside keeps what people typed, if they close it and open it again. If you'd rather it started fresh each time, reset the form when `close` fires.

### Alert dialogs

An alert dialog asks a question that needs an answer before people go on, like whether to delete a project. Set `role="alertdialog"`, so screen readers announce it as an alert, and read its description straight away.

::dialog-alert-example
::framework-switcher
#react
```tsx
<Button color="error" variant="outline" onClick={() => setOpen(true)}>Delete project</Button>
<Dialog
  id="delete-project"
  role="alertdialog"
  size="sm"
  title="Delete project?"
  description="This deletes the project and its 12 files for everyone. You can't undo it."
  open={open}
  onClose={() => setOpen(false)}
  actions={
    <>
      <Button variant="outline" onClick={() => setOpen(false)}>Keep project</Button>
      <Button color="error" onClick={deleteProject}>Delete project</Button>
    </>
  }
/>
```

An alert dialog works a little differently, since a stray click shouldn't count as an answer:

- It has no close button, and a press on the backdrop doesn't close it. `Escape` still does, as the APG asks, which is why it needs an action that leaves things as they are, like "Keep project".
- Focus starts on that action, the least destructive one, so a quick `Enter` never deletes anything. Mark it with `data-autofocus`, or, when the body has no controls, put it first, since the Dialog then starts on the first action.
- Name both actions by what they do, like "Keep project" and "Delete project", rather than "Cancel" and "OK", so people know what they're agreeing to without reading the question again.

Keep alert dialogs for what can't be undone. For everyday actions, like archiving a message, it's kinder to act straight away and offer undo, as the Button's [Make destructive actions recoverable](/docs/components/button/ux#make-destructive-actions-recoverable) explains, since people soon learn to press through a confirmation without reading it.

## Adaptive

### Pointer, touch and pen

A press on the backdrop asks to close a dialog, but only when it starts and ends there. That way, selecting text in the dialog and letting go outside it doesn't close it, and neither does a scroll gesture that starts on the panel.

### Small screens

On a narrow screen, the dialog fills the width, less 16px on each side, and on a short one, the height, with the body scrolling between the title and the actions. On phones, the on-screen keyboard covers the bottom of the screen too, so check that a focused input in a dialog stays in view above it.

### Motion

The dialog fades in and grows from 96% as it opens, over 150ms. It only does when people haven't asked for less motion (`prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference`), and opens straight away otherwise ([WCAG 2.3.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html)). It closes straight away either way, since people have already moved on.

### More contrast

When someone asks for more contrast (`prefers-contrast: more`), as with Increase contrast on macOS and iOS, the panel's border is drawn in the text colour at full strength, rather than at 20%.

### Forced colours

Windows contrast themes replace every colour with the user's own. The panel's background and text become the theme's own, and its border stays, in the theme's text colour, which is what sets the panel apart from the page.

### Colour scheme

The panel's background is `Canvas`, and its text reads tokens that use `light-dark()`, and both follow `color-scheme`, so the Dialog adapts to your light and dark themes on its own, as long as they set it.

### Text size and spacing

Sizes are in `rem`, so the panel grows with its text, at 200% text size ([WCAG 1.4.4](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/resize-text.html)) and with wider line, letter and word spacing ([WCAG 1.4.12](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/text-spacing.html)). A long title wraps rather than getting cut off, and the body scrolls when there's no more room.

### Languages and direction

The Dialog uses logical properties, so the close button moves to the left in right-to-left languages, and the actions start from the right. Translate the close button's label with `closeLabel`, like `closeLabel="إغلاق"` in Arabic, since screen readers read it and voice control users say it.

## FAQ

### Dialog or something else?

A dialog interrupts people, so it's best kept for what needs an answer before they go on. For anything else, another component will do a better job:

| When you need to…                                                   | Use                                     |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| Ask a question people answer before they go on, like a delete       | Dialog, with `role="alertdialog"`       |
| Edit something without leaving the page, like a profile             | Dialog                                  |
| Show more next to what opened it, without blocking the page         | Popover                                 |
| Offer a few actions behind one button                               | Dropdown Menu                           |
| Confirm that something happened, like "Changes saved"               | Toast                                   |
| Edit something with many fields, or that people come back to       | A page of its own                       |

The Popover, the Dropdown Menu and the Toast are on our [Roadmap](/docs/getting-started/roadmap), and they'll share the Dialog's rules for focus, `Escape` and the top layer.

::accordion
:::accordion-item{label="Can a dialog open another dialog?"}
Yes, one at a time. The second one opens on top, in the top layer, with its own backdrop, and makes the first one inert while it's open, just as the first one did the page. When it closes, focus goes back to the control in the first dialog that opened it, which makes it a good fit for a "Discard changes?" alert dialog on top of a form. More than two deep gets hard to follow, so try to keep to that.
:::

:::accordion-item{label="Should the button that opens a dialog say so?"}
It's a good idea to end its label with "…", like "Rename…", which tells people there's more to fill in before anything happens. You can also set `aria-haspopup="dialog"`, which some screen readers announce, although not all, so the label is what everyone gets.
:::
::
